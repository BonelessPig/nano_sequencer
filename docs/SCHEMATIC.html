nano_sequencer schematic

What is wired to the Arduino Nano today, and which pin of each chip it goes to. It follows the firmware in adapters/target/; when a pin or part changes there, change it here too.

  • SPI bus, and the load and latch line
  • Outputs and step lights
  • Analog
  • Clock and reset inputs
  • Panel switches and the run button
  • 5 V
  • Ground
  • Not connected or free

The whole board

Signals only. Every chip also takes 5 V and ground from the Nano, which is powered over USB.

Not drawn above yet: the tenth 74HC165 with the selector, the level light on D6 and the modulation input on A7. Their pins are in the tables below, under "Selector, level light and modulation input".

74HC165 shift register (ten of them)

Chip k of the chain, counting from 0 at the chip nearest the Nano. It reads the four switches of step 2k on inputs H to E and those of step 2k+1 on D to A, the higher letter being the more significant bit. A switch that is on must read high. That holds for chips 0 to 7. Chip 8 reads the eight mode switches instead and is drawn on its own below, and chip 9, the far end of the chain, reads the selector.

Chip 8 reads mode switch 0 on input A up to mode switch 7 on input H, wired as the step switches are: on must read high. The low three, on A, B and C, choose the engine as a number: 0 is the plain engine, 1 the stage engine, 2 Klee, 3 chance, 4 Cartesian and 5 Euclidean; 6 and 7 have no engine yet and play the plain one. Mode switches 3 to 7 belong to the engine chosen, each engine giving them its own meaning.

Chip 9 is wired as chip 8 is, with its serial output to chip 8's serial input and its own serial input to ground. Its inputs A, B and C take the three lines of the selector, a coded rotary switch of eight positions, A being the least significant bit, and a line must read high for a 1. Input D takes the profile button, a momentary push button to 5 V with a pull-down, so that it reads high while it is down. Inputs E to H have nothing on them and go to ground. One read of the chain is 80 bits, ten bytes, and the selector's is the last byte to arrive.

74HC595 shift register (two of them): the step lights

Sixteen LEDs, one for each step, on the outputs of two chips in a chain. The chain is on the SPI bus, sharing its clock and its data out with the DAC: D13 (SCK) goes to SRCLK of both, D11 (MOSI) to SER of the near chip, and QH′ of the near chip to SER of the far one. The near chip's QA to QH are the LEDs of steps 0 to 7 and the far chip's those of steps 8 to 15. A high output lights its LED. O̅E̅ is tied to ground and S̅R̅C̅L̅R̅ to 5 V, so the outputs always drive and nothing clears the chain.

The latch clock, RCLK of both chips, is D9 (PB1): the same pin as the 74HC165 load line. Before each read of the panel the firmware sends two bytes, steps 15 to 8 and then steps 7 to 0, and the rising edge that ends the load pulse puts them on the outputs. The chain has no chip select, so the ten bytes of the panel read and the DAC's frames pass through its shift registers too, but none of that shows: the outputs change only at that edge, once every 8 ms.

The firmware lights the LED of the step that last began, for the first half of the step, which is while the clock output is high. On the Klee engine it lights every active stage of the register instead. Nothing is lit while the pattern is stopped or in calibration mode. A light follows its step by up to one 8 ms read of the panel.

Each LED has its own series resistor, from the output to the LED's anode, and its cathode goes to ground. The resistor is about 470 Ω to 1 kΩ, to be chosen for the LEDs used.

MCP4822 DAC

Channel A is the pitch CV and channel B the envelope CV, both at a gain of 2 from the internal 2.048 V reference, so one step is 1 mV. With LDAC tied to ground the output changes as chip select rises.

Selector, level light and modulation input

One pot sets eight amounts, and the selector says which. None of these three is wired on the board yet, and the modulation input's stage is not designed.

  • Selector. Position 0 is the tempo. Positions 1 to 5 are how much of the LFO moves the note, the gate length, the chance of a step dropping out, which step is played and how loud a note is. Position 6 is the envelope's time, and position 7 how much of the LFO bends the pitch (vibrato). With chip 9 not wired the firmware reads position 0, so the pot is the tempo.
  • The pot. After the selector moves, the pot changes nothing until it has been turned to meet the amount that position holds.
  • Level light. As bright as the amount the selector is on, by pulse width, 977 times a second. Dark at 0.
  • Modulation input. Read on seven ticks in eight, used as each step begins, and on every tick by the vibrato. At half of 5 V it moves nothing.

Clock and reset inputs and run button

Three inputs, each with the chip's internal pull-up switched on, so each pin rests high and something outside pulls it low. The firmware looks at all three once a millisecond. D3, where the sync switch used to be, is free; its pull-up is still on.

Nano pinChip pinWhatWiredPin is low
D8PB0 (ICP1)Clock input jack Through an inverting input stageWhile the jack is high
D2PD2 (INT0)Reset input jack Through an inverting input stageWhile the jack is high
D7PD7Run button, a push button Between the pin and groundWhile it is pressed
  • Clock input. The first edge takes the pattern over from the internal clock, and it stays on this clock until it is stopped. A rising edge at the jack is a falling edge at the pin, which Timer/Counter1's input capture flag latches however short the pulse; the firmware reads and clears the flag on its next tick. No interrupt is used. With the tempo amount in the middle a step begins on every edge; turned down it takes every second, third or fourth edge, and turned up it fits two, three or four steps to an edge.
  • Reset input. A rising edge at the jack sends the pattern back to its first step, which plays when the next step is due. The falling edge at the pin is latched in the INT0 flag and read the same way, with the interrupt left off. Ignored while the pattern is stopped.
  • Run button. Each press stops the pattern or starts it again from its first step. The board powers on running, on the internal clock. A stop forgets the external clock: the start after it is on the internal one, unless an edge came while the pattern was stopped, in which case the start waits for the next.

The switch and the button need no resistor and no capacitor: the pull-ups are inside the chip, and the firmware waits for a contact to hold still for 20 ms before it believes it.

Input stage for the two jacks: suggested, not built

The same circuit twice, one for the clock input and one for the reset input. It has not been built or tested. What the firmware does rely on is that the stage inverts: the pin is low while the jack is high.

  • An NPN transistor of the 2N3904 or BC547 class: emitter to ground, collector to the pin (D8 for the clock, D2 for the reset).
  • The collector is pulled up by the chip's internal pull-up, which the firmware switches on. An external 10 kΩ from the pin to 5 V is an option if a firmer high level or a faster rise is wanted.
  • The base is fed from the jack tip through 100 kΩ, which limits the current into it.
  • A small-signal diode (1N4148) from ground to the base, its cathode at the base, clamps a negative voltage at the jack so the base is never driven far below ground.
  • The jack sleeve goes to ground.

Check before wiring

  • The pin numbers here have been checked against Texas Instruments' SN74HC165 datasheet (SCLS116H, the D, N, NS and PW packages), its SN74HC595 datasheet (SCLS041J, the 16-pin D, N, NS, DB and PW packages), Microchip's MCP4802/4812/4822 datasheet (DS20002249B, the PDIP, SOIC and MSOP packages) and Arduino's Nano pinout sheet. A part from another maker, or in another package, is worth a look at its own.
  • How the panel switches are wired is not recorded anywhere in the repo, the mode switches included. The firmware only needs an input to be high when its switch is on, and a 74HC165 input must never float: a switch to 5 V with a pull-down resistor on each input does both.
  • The tempo pot's value is not recorded either. Its ends go to 5 V and ground because the ADC's reference is AVcc.
  • Only the DAC's 100 nF decoupling capacitor is written down. One across the supply pins of each 74HC165 and each 74HC595 is normal practice.
  • The step lights' series resistor has not been chosen: about 470 Ω to 1 kΩ, to suit the LEDs used. A 74HC595 may carry no more than 70 mA through its VCC or GND pin, and 35 mA at any one output (absolute maximum ratings, SCLS041J), and the Klee engine can light all eight LEDs of a chip at once, so keep each LED to a few milliamps.
  • One pin, D9, serving as both the 74HC165 load line and the 74HC595 latch clock has not been tried on hardware, and nor have the step lights at all: they have only run on the emulated board.
  • The 1 kΩ in the MISO line lets an ISP programmer, which uses D11 to D13, win against the chain's output. The serial bootloader does not need it.
  • The input stage for the clock and reset jacks is a suggestion that has not been built or tested: its part values, the level at which it switches and how it stands up to the voltages a modular system puts out are all still to be found on the bench. Do not wire a jack straight to D8 or D2.
  • That the two edge flags latch with their interrupts off is taken from the ATmega328P datasheet and from the emulated board, whose model of it is this project's own. It has not been seen on a real chip yet.
  • The run button goes from its pin to ground with nothing else: no resistor, and no connection to 5 V.
  • Do not patch the gate, clock or pitch CV into other equipment until their buffer stages are built: nothing protects those pins (OPEN_ITEMS.md, items 9 and 10).
  • A1 (PC1) is configured as an output although nothing uses it. Leave it unconnected.
