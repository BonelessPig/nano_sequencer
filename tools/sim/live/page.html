The step lights and the outline
Light on a step The board's own step LED, as the chip last latched it into the two 74HC595s. Lit for the first half of a step; dark while stopped and in calibration mode.
Outline round a step The step counted by this viewer, which the labels under the steps are worked out from. Where it and the light disagree, the light is right.
How the outlined step is counted
CaseWhat is counted
Any engine Pulses of the clock output, one a step. Back to 0 after a start or a reset made here, and after a change of engine.
Debug image Its log also names each step, and that is believed over the count.
Release and midi images No log, so the count is all there is. A reset that lands within a tick or two of a step can leave it out. MIDI out names notes, not steps.

The engine switch and the mode switches
Engine switch One rotary switch of eight positions that puts out its position as a three-bit number, on mode inputs 2 to 0 of the ninth 74HC165. The code is shown beside the engine's name. Positions 6 and 7 have no engine yet and play the plain one.
Mode switches 7 to 3 Five toggles on the same chip, read on every step. They belong to the engine chosen: the legend over them names each one, or each group read together as a number, and says how it stands. Dashed means no use on this engine.
When they count The engine is taken as each step begins, and a change of engine starts the new one's pattern from its beginning.
About the plain engine
Steps Sixteen, played in order, one a step.
A step's switches Its note as a number, 0 to 15: 0 is a rest, and the figure under the switches is the pitch played, one below the number.
Mode switchesNo use.
About the stage engine
Top row, steps 0 to 7 The notes of eight stages.
Bottom row, steps 8 to 15 Their controls, each under its stage.
Control switches 2 and 1 The stage's length, 1 to 4 steps.
Control switches 8 and 4 Its gate mode. Single: the note on the stage's first step only. Repeat: on every step. Hold: once, for the whole stage. Rest: not at all.
Outline The stage counted as playing, with its control, which counts its steps.
LightOn the stage's note.
Mode switchesNo use.
About the Klee engine
Steps The sixteen stages of a register, which a pattern of bits goes round, one stage a step.
Switch 8 The stage's pattern switch. It counts only at a load, which copies all sixteen into the register.
Switches 4, 2, 1 The stage's value, 0 to 7, read on every step.
Note The sum of the values under the active stages, 15 at most. A sum of 0 is a rest.
Load (3) On the first step, and on the step after the switch is moved either way.
Invert (4) Turns the bit over as it comes round again.
Merge (5) Ties every note to the next step.
Chance (7, 6) How often in 8 the bit coming round is flipped at random, 0 to 3. At 0 the pattern is locked.
Lights The register itself: every active stage is lit, so the pattern can be seen going round. They come from the chip.
Counted here Only how far the register has moved since its load, 0 to 15 and round again, shown under the mode switches. No step is outlined.
About the chance engine
Steps Played as the plain engine plays them, but some are skipped and some picked at random.
Skip (4, 3) How often in 4 a step becomes a rest, 0 to 3.
Shuffle (5) Each step is picked at random.
Outline, shuffle on Debug image: from the log, which names the step. Release and midi images: none, there being no log.
Lights Show the step on both images.
About the Cartesian engine
Steps A grid of four rows of four: step n is at column x = n mod 4 of row y = n ÷ 4. Each half of a panel row is one row of the grid.
Plays The step at column x of row y. Both start at 0 and go round from 3 to 0.
x (4, 3) x moves on every 1, 2, 3 or 4 steps.
y (6, 5) y moves on every 4, 3, 2 or 1 steps.
All four off The sixteen steps in order. Other settings trace other paths, some of which never reach every step.
About the Euclidean engine
Steps Played in order, but a step sounds only on a beat; any other is a rest.
Beats (6 to 3) 1 to 16 in a bar of sixteen, spread as evenly as the bar allows, the first on step 0.
"beat" under a step A beat at the present setting. It is still a rest if the step's own switches are all off.
Sets steps 12 to 15 to 10, 5, 10, 5 and power cycles the board.

Input jacks

Back to the first step, at the next step. Ignored while stopped.
What the inputs do
Clock input (D8) Followed only with the sync switch on. A step then begins on its edges, and with no edges nothing plays.
Tempo pot, sync on Sets how many edges to a step or steps to an edge, ÷4 to ×4.
Run button (D7) Each press stops the pattern, or starts it again from the first step.
Reset input (D2) Sends the pattern back to its first step, which plays when the next step is due. Ignored while stopped.
Power cycle Leaves the sync switch and the clock source as they are. The board comes up running. Choosing an image is a power cycle with that image.
The clock patterns, and what the firmware makes of an uneven clock
PatternEdges
SteadyOne every period.
Swing In pairs, a long gap then a short one, a pair lasting two periods. The figure is the long gap's share: 50% would be steady, 67% is triplet swing and 75% dotted.
Gated white noise Each period is a slot with an even chance of an edge, whatever came before: gaps of 1, 2, 3 or more slots with no pattern to them.
Gated pink noise A slot has an edge while a slowly wandering noise is over its middle: runs of edges with lulls between.
Both random ones An edge every 2 slots on average and never more than 16 apart. The train is the same each time the clock is started.
On an uneven clockThe firmware
Every edge Is seen once a millisecond, so its step begins a tick or two after it.
Gate and clock output Are high for half the gap before the edge, not half the gap after it, which is not known yet.
An edge in under half the last gap Finds the gate and clock output still high. Its step waits a tick with both low, then begins.
The first edge, or one after 4 s of none Has no gap to go by, so its step is taken to be 20 ms long: a 10 ms gate and clock pulse.
× ratios The steps between two edges are spaced by the gap before, so they bunch or stretch after a change.

Calibration mode

The sequence is stopped. The gate is held high and the pitch CV sits at one point of the calibration table, to be measured.

Step 0's switches Pick the point, 0 to 4: one per octave.
Tempo pot Trims its voltage, a millivolt at a time.
Step 15's lowest switch Off and on again stores it. The gate drops for a quarter of a second to show it did.
To leave Change any switch on steps 12 to 15, then power cycle. Keep the EEPROM to play from what was stored.

                The rest of what is shown
                
Times
                    Chip time at which the message's first byte began. A
                        byte takes 320 µs and each waits for the one ahead,
                        so a Note On begins about 0.4 ms after its gate
                        rises, and 0.7 ms behind a Start.

                
Sounding
                    The notes given a Note On and no Note Off yet, as a
                        receiver would have them. A watchdog reset sends no
                        Note Off, so a note it cut short stays here until
                        that note is next played and ended.

                
Transport
                    A Start or a Stop says whether the pattern is
                        running, and is believed over the count of presses
                        of the run button, as the debug image's Transport
                        line is. It does not say which clock.

                
Calibration mode
                    Nothing is sent.

                
Debug and release images
                    Neither sends MIDI: the debug image's serial pin
                        carries its log and the release image's is never
                        switched on.

                
Not emulated
                    Only the bytes are. The MIDI out circuit, and what a
                        receiver makes of the bytes, are not.

            The watchdog, the crash log and what the button does
            

                
Watchdog
                    A timer in the chip that resets it unless the
                        firmware kicks it, which it does once a tick. It
                        times out after about 256 ms.

                
Stop the tick
                    The firmware never hangs by itself, so this stops
                        its tick from outside: the loop goes on round with
                        nothing to do, which is place 5. No other place can
                        be reached from here.

                
After the reset
                    The firmware starts as at power-on: it reads the
                        panel afresh, comes up running and plays from the
                        first step. The debug image's start-up line reads
                        Reset = 8, and the midi image sends Start again.

                
Crash log
                    Eight records in the EEPROM, used in turn. One is
                        written after each watchdog reset, about 18 ms into
                        the start-up. It is read here from the EEPROM, not
                        from the serial log, so the release and midi images
                        show it too.

                
Record
                    Its number: 1 for the first, and round from 255 to
                        0. The newest is the one not followed by the next
                        number.

                
Cause
                    The reset flags, as in the start-up line: 8 is the
                        watchdog.

                
Place
                    What the firmware was doing: 1 past start-up with no
                        tick run yet, 2 applying outputs, 3 gathering
                        inputs, 4 in the core, 5 between ticks.

                
Count
                    How many times running the same crash happened, 1 to
                        255. A crash with the cause and place of the newest
                        record raises its count; any other takes the next
                        slot.

                
To clear it
                    Untick "Keep the EEPROM" and power cycle, which
                        erases the calibration too. Nothing else clears
                        it.

            How to use calibration mode
            
The mode sets the table pitch is played from: five points,
                one per octave, each the DAC value that should give exactly
                0, 1, 2, 3 and 4 V. On a real board each is trimmed against
                a voltmeter. Here the DAC is ideal, so the Pitch CV figure
                stands in for the meter and nothing needs correcting, but a
                point can be stored off its mark to see it take effect.

            

                
StepDoExpect

                
1. Enter
                    Click "Enter calibration mode".
                    The sequence stops, the gate stays high and the
                        pitch CV holds still: with the sound on, one steady
                        tone.

                
2. Pick a point
                    Set step 0's switches to 0, 1, 2, 3 or 4 (switches
                        1, 2 and 4; a higher value counts as 4).
                    Point n should read n volts.

                
3. Trim it
                    Turn the tempo pot until the Pitch CV reads the
                        voltage wanted.
                    The middle of the pot (about 512) is the point's
                        nominal value. Its travel covers 64 mV below to
                        63 mV above, a millivolt for every 8 of the reading.
                        Point 0 cannot go below 0 V.

                
4. Store it
                    Switch step 15's lowest switch (marked 1) off and on
                        again.
                    The gate drops for a quarter of a second, a gap in
                        the tone, and the debug image logs the point and the
                        value. It is written to the EEPROM there and then.

                
5. Repeat
                    Do the same for any other points.
                    A point that is not stored keeps the value it had.

                
6. Leave
                    Change any switch on steps 12 to 15, then click
                        "Power cycle" with "Keep the EEPROM" ticked.
                    The debug image's first log line says Cal = 1, and
                        notes are played from the stored table, on the
                        straight line between the octaves either side.

                
Back to the ideal table
                    Untick "Keep the EEPROM" and power cycle.
                    The EEPROM is erased and the first log line says
                        Cal = 0.
