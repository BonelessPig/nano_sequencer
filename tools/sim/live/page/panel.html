The step lights and the outline
Light on a step The board's own step LED, as the chip last latched it into the two 74HC595s. Lit for the first half of a step; dark while stopped and in calibration mode.
Outline round a step The step counted by this viewer, which the labels under the steps are worked out from. Where it and the light disagree, the light is right.
How the outlined step is counted
CaseWhat is counted
Any engine Pulses of the clock output, one a step. Back to 0 after a start or a reset made here, and after a change of engine.
Debug image Its log also names each step, and that is believed over the count.
Release and midi images No log, so the count is all there is. A reset that lands within a tick or two of a step can leave it out. MIDI out names notes, not steps.
A switch moved Counted from the panel read at which the chip took it, so it does not put the count out.
Stage The log names the stage. Which of its steps is playing is always counted, from the lengths as they stood when the stage began.
Klee How far the register has moved since its last load; back to 0 when mode switch 3 is moved.
Cartesian The two counters, as the firmware counts them, from the mode switches as each step begins.
Chance, shuffle on Nothing to count: only the log or the lights can say which step was picked.
Random numbers The firmware's are not followed here.

The engine switch and the mode switches
Engine switch One rotary switch of eight positions that puts out its position as a three-bit number, on mode inputs 2 to 0 of the ninth 74HC165. The code is shown beside the engine's name. Positions 6 and 7 have no engine yet and play the plain one.
Mode switches 7 to 3 Five toggles on the same chip, read on every step. They belong to the engine chosen: the legend over them names each one, or each group read together as a number, and says how it stands. Dashed means no use on this engine.
When they count The engine is taken as each step begins, and a change of engine starts the new one's pattern from its beginning.
About the plain engine
Steps Sixteen, played in order, one a step.
A step's switches Its note as a number, 0 to 15: 0 is a rest, and the figure under the switches is the pitch played, one below the number.
Mode switchesNo use.
About the stage engine
Top row, steps 0 to 7 The notes of eight stages.
Bottom row, steps 8 to 15 Their controls, each under its stage.
Control switches 2 and 1 The stage's length, 1 to 4 steps.
Control switches 8 and 4 Its gate mode. Single: the note on the stage's first step only. Repeat: on every step. Hold: once, for the whole stage. Rest: not at all.
Outline The stage counted as playing, with its control, which counts its steps.
LightOn the stage's note.
Mode switchesNo use.
About the Klee engine
Steps The sixteen stages of a register, which a pattern of bits goes round, one stage a step.
Switch 8 The stage's pattern switch. It counts only at a load, which copies all sixteen into the register.
Switches 4, 2, 1 The stage's value, 0 to 7, read on every step.
Note The sum of the values under the active stages, 15 at most. A sum of 0 is a rest.
Load (3) On the first step, and on the step after the switch is moved either way.
Invert (4) Turns the bit over as it comes round again.
Merge (5) Ties every note to the next step.
Chance (7, 6) How often in 8 the bit coming round is flipped at random, 0 to 3. At 0 the pattern is locked.
Lights The register itself: every active stage is lit, so the pattern can be seen going round. They come from the chip.
Counted here Only how far the register has moved since its load, 0 to 15 and round again, shown under the mode switches. No step is outlined.
About the chance engine
Steps Played as the plain engine plays them, but some are skipped and some picked at random.
Skip (4, 3) How often in 4 a step becomes a rest, 0 to 3.
Shuffle (5) Each step is picked at random.
Outline, shuffle on Debug image: from the log, which names the step. Release and midi images: none, there being no log.
Lights Show the step on both images.
About the Cartesian engine
Steps A grid of four rows of four: step n is at column x = n mod 4 of row y = n ÷ 4. Each half of a panel row is one row of the grid.
Plays The step at column x of row y. Both start at 0 and go round from 3 to 0.
x (4, 3) x moves on every 1, 2, 3 or 4 steps.
y (6, 5) y moves on every 4, 3, 2 or 1 steps.
All four off The sixteen steps in order. Other settings trace other paths, some of which never reach every step.
About the Euclidean engine
Steps Played in order, but a step sounds only on a beat; any other is a rest.
Beats (6 to 3) 1 to 16 in a bar of sixteen, spread as evenly as the bar allows, the first on step 0.
"beat" under a step A beat at the present setting. It is still a rest if the step's own switches are all off.

The selector, the pot and the LFO
Selector An 8-way coded rotary switch that says which of eight amounts the pot sets. Position 0 is the tempo. Positions 1 to 5 each hold how much of the LFO goes to one thing; 0 is off. Position 6 is the envelope's time. Position 7 is how much of the LFO goes to the vibrato.
Profile A button that keeps eight sets of the seven amounts at positions 1 to 7 (not the tempo) in the chip's EEPROM, one set for each selector position. What it does is decided as it is let go: a click recalls the selected set, a hold of 1 s saves to it, of 5 s clears it and of 10 s clears all eight. The selected set is loaded at power-on if it holds anything. After a recall the pot has to meet the amount again. Keep the EEPROM over a power cycle to see them come back.
LFO rate A second pot, for an LFO of the board's own: a sine, off with the pot right down, then from a cycle a minute to about 20 Hz at the top, with the slow rates taking most of the travel. Its swing is added to whatever is at the LFO input, and the sum goes no further than either end. It runs free: a stop or a start does not begin its cycle again.
Pot After the selector moves, the pot changes nothing until it has been turned to meet the amount that position holds. So a position passed through on the way is left alone, and to give a new position an amount the pot goes down to 0 and then up.
Level light As bright as the amount the selector is on, so it shows where the pot has to go to meet it.
LFO input The reading the chip takes from the modulation input's stage, used as each step begins, and all the time by the vibrato. At its centre, 512, which is also what it reads with nothing plugged in, it moves nothing.
1 Note Moves each note by up to seven places in the scale, up while the LFO is above its centre and down while below. Never makes or moves a rest.
2 Gate length Makes each gate up to half a step longer, where it ties into the next note, or shorter, down to one clock pulse.
3 Step chance While the LFO is above its centre, makes steps drop out at random, the more the further it goes.
4 Step position Plays another step in place of the one the pattern has got to: up to half the range of steps further on while the LFO is above its centre, or back while below, coming round past the ends. The pattern itself is not moved. The stage engine moves by stages, the Klee engine turns its register, and the Cartesian engine goes round the sixteen. With no log (the release and midi images) the step named here is the pattern's own; the step lights show the one played.
5 Amplitude While the LFO is below its centre, makes notes quieter: the envelope CV's peak comes down from 4.095 V towards 0 V, and the MIDI velocity from 100 towards 1. Above the centre nothing is taken off.
6 Envelope time No LFO in this one. At the bottom of the pot the envelope CV (the DAC's second output, shown under Signals) is the gate at the height of the peak. Above that it rises to the peak as a note begins and falls at once, each taking the time set, up to about a second, whatever the gate length. The pot is finer at the short end: half way is a quarter of a second.
7 Vibrato Bends the pitch CV of the note that is sounding, up to two semitones either way: up while the LFO is above its centre and down while below. It follows the LFO every millisecond, not once a step. The note itself is not moved, and MIDI out sends no pitch bend.
Sets steps 12 to 15 to 10, 5, 10, 5 and power cycles the board.
